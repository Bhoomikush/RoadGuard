# RoadGuard Road Safety Knowledge Base

Welcome to the RoadGuard Knowledge Base. This document provides practical, factual guidance on identifying, navigating, and reporting common road hazards, particularly in the context of Indian road conditions.

## Potholes

Potholes are bowl-shaped depressions in the road surface. They commonly form due to wear and tear combined with water ingress, especially during and after the monsoon season.

*   **Risks:** Potholes can cause severe damage to vehicle suspensions, tires, and alignment. For two-wheelers, they pose a critical risk of loss of balance, skidding, and severe accidents.
*   **Identification:** Look for dark patches on the road, accumulation of water, or vehicles ahead swerving or braking suddenly.
*   **Navigation:** 
    *   If you spot a pothole, slow down smoothly. Avoid sudden, hard braking right at the edge of the pothole, as a locked wheel suffers more damage upon impact.
    *   Do not swerve into oncoming traffic or blind spots to avoid a pothole. It is safer to drive through a pothole slowly than to risk a head-on collision.
    *   Hold the steering wheel or handlebars firmly to maintain control.
*   **Water-filled Potholes:** Never assume the depth of a puddle. A small pool of water can hide a deep crater.

## Road Cracks

Road cracks range from minor surface fissures to deep, structural splits.

*   **Types of Cracks:**
    *   *Alligator Cracking:* Interconnected cracks resembling an alligator's skin, indicating underlying structural failure.
    *   *Longitudinal/Transverse Cracks:* Long cracks running parallel or perpendicular to the road.
*   **Hazards:** While four-wheelers can generally pass over minor cracks safely, they pose a significant threat to two-wheelers and cyclists. Narrow tires can get caught in longitudinal cracks, violently throwing the rider off balance.
*   **Navigation:** Two-wheeler riders should approach longitudinal cracks at an angle rather than parallel to them to prevent the tire from catching in the groove.

## Damaged Road Surfaces

Beyond potholes and cracks, general surface degradation is a frequent hazard.

*   **Uneven Surfaces (Rutting and Corrugation):** Continuous heavy traffic can create grooves (ruts) or a washboard effect (corrugation). These reduce tire traction and make steering unpredictable.
*   **Loose Gravel and Sand:** Often found near construction zones, unpaved shoulders, or where road surfaces have deteriorated. Loose materials drastically reduce friction, leading to longer braking distances and a high risk of skidding, especially when cornering.
*   **Waterlogging:** Poor drainage can lead to flooded road sections. Driving through deep water can stall engines, hide underlying hazards, and cause hydroplaning (where tires lose contact with the road).

## Basic Road Hazard Severity Guidance

When assessing or reporting a road hazard, use the following general severity guidelines:

*   **Low Risk (Minor Hazard):**
    *   Small, shallow potholes (less than a couple of inches deep).
    *   Minor surface cracks.
    *   Faded road markings or slight unevenness.
    *   *Impact:* Minimal danger to four-wheelers if driven over at normal speeds; requires minor caution from two-wheelers.
*   **Medium Risk (Moderate Hazard):**
    *   Moderately deep potholes that cause noticeable jarring.
    *   Large patches of loose gravel or minor waterlogging.
    *   Wide road cracks.
    *   *Impact:* Can cause tire damage if hit at speed. Requires all vehicles to slow down. Poses a notable risk of skidding for two-wheelers.
*   **High Risk (Severe Hazard):**
    *   Deep craters or sinkholes.
    *   Completely washed-out road sections or severe flooding.
    *   Large fallen obstacles (e.g., heavy tree branches, construction debris).
    *   *Impact:* Imminent threat to life and vehicle integrity. High probability of accidents, especially at night. Requires immediate evasive action and reporting.

## Immediate Safety Precautions

When encountering bad road conditions, take these immediate steps to ensure safety:

*   **Reduce Speed:** The single most effective safety measure is driving at a speed appropriate for the road condition, regardless of the official speed limit.
*   **Increase Following Distance:** Bad roads require more reaction time. Leave a larger gap between your vehicle and the one ahead. This gives you time to see hazards the leading vehicle encounters and space to stop safely if they brake suddenly.
*   **Stay Alert:** Avoid all distractions. Continuously scan the road ahead, not just the vehicle directly in front of you.
*   **Avoid Sudden Maneuvers:** Smooth steering and gentle braking maintain vehicle stability. Sudden jerks can lead to a loss of control on compromised surfaces.

## General Road Safety

General best practices for navigating varied road conditions:

*   **Visibility:** Always use appropriate lighting. Use low beams in fog or heavy rain to prevent glare. Ensure your windshield, mirrors, and lights are clean.
*   **Monsoon Driving:** Indian monsoons severely degrade road infrastructure. Assume all flooded areas are hazardous. Ensure tires have adequate tread depth to prevent hydroplaning.
*   **Two-Wheeler Safety:** Riders are most vulnerable to road hazards. Always wear an ISI-certified helmet securely strapped, along with protective clothing.
*   **Vehicle Maintenance:** Keep tires properly inflated. Under-inflated tires are more susceptible to blowout upon impacting a pothole. Ensure brakes and suspension are in good working order.

## Reporting a Road Hazard

Reporting hazards helps authorities fix issues and warns other drivers.

*   **Using RoadGuard:**
    1.  Ensure you are completely stopped in a safe location before using the app.
    2.  Capture a clear photograph of the hazard. Try to include some surrounding context (like a landmark or the road edge) to help authorities locate it.
    3.  Allow the app to access your GPS location for accurate mapping.
    4.  Submit the report. The AI will assist in determining the hazard type and severity.
*   **Safe Reporting Practices:** 
    *   **NEVER** attempt to capture photos, record videos, or operate the reporting app while driving or riding. 
    *   If you spot a hazard while moving, pull over safely to the side of the road, turn on your hazard lights, and then file the report. Do not stand in the middle of active traffic to take a photo.
